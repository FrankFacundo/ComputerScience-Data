## The short answer

**Take the leasing.** On the reference car (BMW iX2 eDrive20, the cheapest on the list) the company car costs you **182.45 €/month** out of pocket. Buying the same car with the cash alternative costs **807.39 €/month** net of everything, including the resale value you keep at the end.

Over 36 months: **6 568 €** versus **29 066 €**. The gap is **22 498 €**.

Three things in your reasoning move the answer, and all three move it the same way:

1. **You can't deduct 200 €/month of loan payments.** Luxembourg caps deductible debt interest at **672 €/year** (*intérêts débiteurs*, special expenses) — and that ceiling is *shared* with your insurance premiums. Capital repayment is never deductible. The real saving is about **280 €/year**, not 2 400 €/year.
2. **The company car is barely taxed right now.** An electric company car is a benefit in kind of only **0.5 %/month** of list price until the end of 2026. On the iX2 that's 262 €/month of notional income, costing you 130.88 €/month in tax and social security. Not zero — but very cheap.
3. **The 535 € is worth much less as cash than as a car.** 535 € gross gives you **268.25 € net** (50.1 %), while as a leasing budget it buys 535 € of car, insurance, maintenance, tyres and road tax at full value.

Your flexibility argument is the real one, and it survives the maths — it just isn't worth the gap. See §5.

## 2. What the company car actually costs you

In Luxembourg you are not taxed on the rental the employer pays. You are taxed on a **benefit in kind (BIK)**: a fixed monthly percentage of the car's list price when new, options and VAT included, discounts deducted. It is treated as salary, so it carries both income tax **and** social security contributions.

| Vehicle type | Registered ≤ 31 Dec 2026 | Registered from 1 Jan 2027 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100 % electric, ≤ 18 kWh/100 km | **0.5 %** | 1.0 % |
| 100 % electric, > 18 kWh/100 km | **0.6 %** | 1.2 % |
| Combustion / hybrid | 2.0 % | 2.0 % |

> **This is a dated opportunity.** The 0.5 / 0.6 % rate is locked in for the whole life of the contract if the car is registered by 31 Dec 2026 — or by 31 Dec 2027 where the contract was signed before 31 Dec 2026. Sign in 2026 and you keep the cheap rate for the full 36 months. Sign in 2027 and your BIK **doubles**.

**The company car is 22 498 € cheaper over 36 months for the identical vehicle.**

The reason is not the tax trick — it is that the employer is spending **17 496 €** on this car over three years and you are taxed on a benefit of only 9 450 €. If you take cash instead, that 17 496 € shrinks to 9 657 € in your hands, and you then have to fund the same car — including its depreciation, which is the single largest line in car ownership and the one the leasing company is absorbing for you.

## 4. The 200 €/month credit idea, priced properly

At 5.5 % over 60 months, **200 €/month borrows 10 471 €**. That is the entire budget — before insurance, tyres, maintenance and road tax, which the leasing covers and which run about 1 680 €/year on a car of this class.

To buy the BMW iX2 eDrive20 M Edition on the same terms you would pay **1 002.81 €/month**, not 200 €.

### The deduction that isn't there

| | Your assumption | Luxembourg reality |

The *intérêts débiteurs* ceiling is 672 €/year per person (× spouse, × child). It is a **shared** ceiling with your deductible insurance premiums — if you already claim those, the car loan adds **nothing**. Set `interest_premium_ceiling_used` in the app to test that.

*Both columns assume the car budget stops when you leave; the buying column is the same purchase run for a shorter horizon, so its resale value is higher and its loan balance larger.*

Even leaving at month 6 and eating half the remaining rentals, the leasing still wins on cost. The lock-in is a **liquidity and optionality** problem, not a pricing one: it is a lump sum you may have to find at the worst possible moment — the month you change job.

**Get the exact early-termination clause in writing before signing.** Ask specifically: (a) what happens if you resign, (b) what happens if you are made redundant, (c) whether the new employer can take over the contract, (d) the per-km penalty above the mileage cap. Policies vary from *employer absorbs everything* to *employee owes 100 % of remaining rentals*, and the difference is thousands of euros. The default here (50 %) is a guess — replace it.

### Ownership really does give you something

- You can sell any day, at any price, to anyone.
- No mileage cap, no return-condition inspection, no wear-and-tear bill.
- The car survives a job change.
- At month 36 you hold **3 508 €** of net equity.

None of that is free — the table in §3 prices it at 22 498 € over three years, or about 625 €/month. That is what you are paying for flexibility. Whether it is worth it is a judgement call, not a calculation — but it is a large premium.

## 6. When does buying actually win?

Break-even: buying beats the company car only if the car you buy costs **≤ 16 027 €**.

Above 16 027 €, the leasing is cheaper. Below it, you are no longer comparing like with like — you are choosing to drive a much more modest car and keep the difference. That is a legitimate choice, but it is a **lifestyle** decision, not an arbitrage.

## 8. Recommendation

1. **Take the leasing, and sign it in 2026.** The 0.5 % electric BIK rate is locked for the contract's life if you register by 31 Dec 2026 (or sign by then and register by 31 Dec 2027). From 2027 it doubles to 1 %. That deadline is worth more than any car choice on the list.
2. **Stay at or under the 535 € budget.** The iX2 and iX1 are the only two that do. Everything above is post-tax money.
3. **Get three numbers in writing before you sign:** the *valeur à neuf TTC* (drives your BIK), the WLTP consumption (0.5 % vs 0.6 %), and the early-termination clause (your entire downside).
4. **Drop the loan-interest deduction from your reasoning.** It is worth about 280 €/year at best, and zero if you already use the ceiling on insurance premiums.
5. **Revisit at renewal, not now.** If you expect to leave BNP within a year, or you want a cheap car and the cash, buying is defensible — but then buy a ~15–20 k€ used EV, not a 55 k€ one. Buying the same car as the leasing is the one option that is clearly dominated.
